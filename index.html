Ingredient Costing
What is Ingredient Cost? ▸

The direct cost of the raw ingredients used in one batch of this recipe — what you paid for flour, sugar, butter, and everything else that goes into it.

Automatic unit conversion works within weight (oz/lb/g/kg) or within volume (tsp…L). Weight and volume can't convert to each other because that depends on ingredient density — if you mix them, we'll flag it instead of guessing.

Overhead
What is Overhead? ▸

The cost of running your business that isn't tied to one specific batch — electricity, insurance, software, rent. It's real, even though no single ingredient represents it.

For newer bakers who don't know their exact monthly overhead yet, this applies a simple allowance for utilities, equipment wear, software, insurance and other operating expenses, on top of ingredients + packaging + labor.

Transaction & Selling Costs
What are Transaction Fees? ▸

Money taken out of each sale before it reaches you — card processing, marketplace commissions, delivery. They scale with your price, so they change how high a price needs to be to hit your target margin.

Waste / Business Buffer
What is a Buffer? ▸

A general safety margin for the things a per-ingredient waste % doesn't cover: a whole batch that fails, samples you give away, or ingredient prices creeping up before your next order.

This is separate from any ingredient-specific waste % (which already accounts for trim/spoilage on that one ingredient) — this buffer covers bigger-picture business risk instead.

Profitability Dashboard
Revenue vs. Profit per hour ▸

Revenue per labor hour is everything customers pay, divided by your hours. Business profit per labor hour is what's left after every cost — including the wage you already paid yourself — divided by hours. Revenue per hour will always look bigger; profit per hour is the real number.
